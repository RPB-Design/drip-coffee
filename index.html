It then drips through the brew basket and down the destratification tube into the carafe. Every water surface is free: its level comes out of the flow, not out of a guess.

How this was computed and what is assumed
  • Brew basket, with a free surface: time-accurate 2D Navier–Stokes with Darcy–Brinkman drag for the coffee bed and filter paper, on a 1 mm grid. The liquid surface is held at atmospheric pressure. Its height comes from the mass balance (spray in minus drip-stop out) and moves freely through the brew. The surface is treated as flat: at these speeds waves are far below 1 mm, and the cost of resolving them would buy nothing. Temperature (with heat loss through the basket wall and surface) and dissolved coffee are carried along with the flow.
  • Calibration: a fixed lid hides how high the water actually stands. With a free surface the bed and paper resistance have to keep the level inside the cone, so they are tuned to a medium grind (bed permeability 3×10⁻¹² m²; paper resistance 2×10⁹ m⁻¹). The bed's share of the flow therefore differs a little from the earlier fixed-lid runs.
  • Reservoir: 2D Navier–Stokes, 20 °C water. The level falls by volume as the heater draws 3.7 mL/s. The tank's taper is from the product photo, with a depth chosen so 1.25 L fills it to the top mark. The flow pattern comes from the mid-brew solution and is shown at each level.
  • Feed tube, copper coil, glass riser, spray arm: a 1D energy balance with a drift-flux two-phase model. A 1,375 W brew element heats 3.7 mL/s from 20 to 100 °C (1,237 W). The rest boils 1.7% of the flow, and that steam lifts the water with no pump. The heater stops when the reservoir is empty, as the real machine does.
  • Carafe: the level follows the cumulative drip-stop flow, using the carafe's round bulb shape. Its temperature and strength are the mixed average of what has arrived. Heat loss through the glass is ignored here; the hotplate then holds the coffee at 79–85 °C.
  • Geometry is traced from a labelled front-view product photo and scaled to the 356 mm (14") height, not measured from a machine. Tracer speeds are log-scaled so that slow and fast regions are both visible. The probe always reports the true value.
